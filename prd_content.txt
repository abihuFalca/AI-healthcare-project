1. Executive Summary

Patients often delay seeking care because they cannot judge how serious their symptoms are, while overloaded clinicians struggle to identify which patients need attention first. This product lets a patient submit symptoms, history, risk factors and vitals; an AI pipeline then produces an explainable risk score and triage priority, which a healthcare worker reviews and confirms.

The platform combines three layers: (1) an NLP layer that converts free-text symptoms into structured data, (2) an interpretable machine-learning model that estimates risk, and (3) a deterministic clinical safety-rule engine that guarantees emergency warning signs are never left to a probabilistic model or an LLM alone.

2. Problem Statement & Opportunity

2.1 Problems

Patients lack a structured, understandable way to gauge urgency; early warning signs are missed or over-reported.

Clinicians have no consistent, data-driven prioritisation of incoming patients; high-risk cases can wait in the same queue as routine ones.

Free-text symptom descriptions are unstructured and hard to use in analytics.

Longitudinal change (e.g. rising blood pressure and heart rate) is rarely visualised in one place.

Existing AI tools are often opaque; clinicians will not trust a score without reasons.

2.2 Opportunity

A transparent, role-aware triage assistant can shorten time-to-attention for high-risk patients, give patients clear next steps, and give clinicians an explainable, reviewable prioritised queue while keeping humans accountable for decisions.

3. Goals, Non-Goals & Success Metrics

3.1 Product Goals

Deliver an end-to-end working prototype across all three roles (Patient, Healthcare Worker, Admin).

Produce a risk score, risk level and triage recommendation for every submitted assessment.

Provide a human-readable explanation (SHAP or equivalent) with every prediction.

Guarantee deterministic emergency-sign detection before any AI scoring.

Give clinicians a prioritised high-risk queue with review, notes and triage override.

Enforce role-based access, consent, and audit logging from day one.

3.2 Non-Goals (MVP)

Diagnosing any disease or prescribing treatment.

Replacing clinician judgement or acting as a regulated medical device.

EHR/HIS integration, teleconsultation, wearables, voice/WhatsApp/IVR access.

Email/SMS/push notifications (in-app only for MVP).

Production-grade regulatory compliance (documented as a requirement for deployment).

6.5 NLP Layer

Example input: "I have been having chest discomfort for three hours and I am sweating."

Expected structured output: symptoms = [chest discomfort, sweating]; duration = 3 hours; severity = unknown.

Approach for MVP: rule/lexicon-based extraction with synonym mapping (e.g. "tight chest" → chest discomfort), negation handling ("no fever"), and duration parsing; optional light spaCy/transformer upgrade.

Extracted symptoms are shown back to the patient for confirmation before analysis.

Unrecognised text is retained verbatim for the clinician; the NLP layer never invents symptoms.

Initial language support: English; multilingual NLP is a future feature (preferred language is captured now).

7.3 Validation & Monitoring

Report accuracy, recall/sensitivity for HIGH, precision, ROC-AUC and calibration on a held-out set; prioritise sensitivity for HIGH risk.

Review fairness across age and gender subgroups.

Risk-band thresholds are configuration, validated with clinical advisors, never presented as universal medical thresholds.

Post-MVP: drift monitoring, periodic retraining, and a model registry.

8. Emergency Safety Layer

A deterministic rule engine runs before and alongside the ML model. An LLM or ML model alone must never determine emergency status.

9.2 Privacy

Explicit consent and a clear privacy notice before the first assessment; consent version and timestamp stored.

Data minimisation: collect only fields needed for risk assessment.

Ability to deactivate an account; data retention and deletion policy documented.

Avoid sending unnecessary personal information to external AI services; strip identifiers if any external call is used.

Prototype disclaimer: production deployment requires compliance with applicable healthcare and privacy regulations and institutional requirements (for example, India's DPDP Act and relevant health-data rules, HIPAA/GDPR where applicable), plus clinical safety assessment.

9.3 Accessibility & UX Quality

Target WCAG 2.1 AA contrast and keyboard navigation.

Risk indicators use colour plus text labels and icons: LOW (green), MODERATE (amber), HIGH (red).

Responsive layout; patient interface optimised for mobile.

Plain-language wording suitable for non-clinical users.

9.4 Performance, Reliability & Maintainability

Inference plus explanation completes within seconds; dashboards load in under 3 seconds with demo data volumes.

Graceful error states (LoadingState / ErrorMessage components); if the AI service fails, the assessment is saved and the patient is advised to contact a clinician rather than shown a guess.

Automated unit tests for NLP, safety rules, validation and RBAC; API integration tests for core flows.

Structured logging without PHI; database migrations under version control.

11.3 Data Integrity Rules

Assessment AI fields (risk_score, risk_level, ai explanations, model_version) are write-once; no role can UPDATE them via the application.

Clinician decisions live in healthcare_reviews; the effective triage shown is the latest review, with the AI value preserved.

Soft-delete / deactivate instead of hard delete for clinically relevant records.

Add a consent record (user_id, consent_version, accepted_at) to support the privacy requirement.

14. Key User Flows

14.1 Patient Assessment

Patient logs in → dashboard → Start New Assessment → consent (first time).

Completes Steps 1–6 with live validation → Step 7 review → Analyze My Risk.

Backend runs validation, emergency check, NLP, model, SHAP, triage and recommendation, saves the assessment and creates notifications.

Patient sees the result page; the assessment appears in history and the timeline.

14.2 Clinician Review

Worker receives a high-risk notification → opens high-risk queue → opens patient details.

Reviews symptoms, vitals, AI explanation and trend → adds clinical note.

Confirms or changes triage status → marks as reviewed or escalated → patient is notified.

14.3 Healthcare Worker Onboarding

Worker registers → status Pending (can log in but sees an "awaiting approval" page).

Admin verifies professional ID → approves → status Approved/Active → full access; action audit-logged.

18. Assumptions, Dependencies & Open Questions

18.1 Assumptions

Users have internet access and a modern browser; mobile web is sufficient (no native app).

English is the initial language of the UI and NLP.

Healthcare-worker assignment is manual or rule-based (e.g. by department) in the MVP.

Patients can self-report vitals (e.g. from a home device); values are not device-verified.

18.2 Open Questions

Which clinical advisor will review safety rules, ranges and risk-band thresholds?

Which target condition(s) does the model predict risk for (general deterioration vs. cardiovascular focus)? The demo suggests a cardiovascular emphasis.

What dataset will be used for training, and under what licence?

How are patients assigned to healthcare workers (by facility, department, round-robin)?

What is the data-retention period and deletion process?

Which jurisdictions and regulations apply for any pilot?
